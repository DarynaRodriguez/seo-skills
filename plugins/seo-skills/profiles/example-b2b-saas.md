# Site profile

A worked example. The company is fictional, the shape is real: a mid-market B2B
SaaS platform selling into two European markets, with a small content team and a
legal function that vetoes claims. Copy this, replace everything, keep the blanks
honest.

Generated by `/seo-profile-setup` on 2026-08-26. Confirmed by the marketing lead.

---

## 1. Site

- **Brand name:** Halden (always capitalised, never HALDEN or halden)
- **Primary domain:** halden.io
- **CMS / platform:** Webflow, blog and case studies in CMS collections
- **Who can publish:** Marta Feldt, Head of Marketing. Nothing goes live without her.
- **Analytics and search data available:** Google Search Console (halden.io, domain property), Ahrefs project #4412, no AI visibility tracking yet

- **Market priority order:** Germany first this year, UK second. The DE pages get the new work.

## 3. Who this is for

- **Primary buyer:** Head of Supply Chain or Head of Procurement, manufacturing companies of 500 to 5,000 employees
- **Secondary buyer or influencer:** IT lead who has to approve the ERP integration, and the CFO who signs above 50k
- **The job they are trying to get done:** stop losing three days a week to chasing suppliers by email, without ripping out SAP
- **What disqualifies a visitor:** companies under 100 employees, agencies, students, anyone looking for free templates
- **How they search:** the problem and the integration, rarely the category. "supplier delivery tracking sap" gets qualified traffic. "supply chain software" gets students.

## 4. What we sell

- **Category we compete in:** supplier collaboration and delivery tracking software
- **What the product actually does:** Halden pulls open purchase orders from the customer's ERP and gives suppliers a portal to confirm, change, or flag delivery dates. Deviations surface in one list instead of an inbox.
- **Proof we can cite:** 3 named customers with signed case studies (Wernicke, Loftgren, Baumann Tech), a measured 62% reduction in delivery-chasing emails at Wernicke over 6 months, ISO 27001, SAP certified integration
- **Claims we may not make:** anything about cost savings in currency, anything implying we replace an ERP, anything about AI making decisions autonomously, any customer name not on the list above

## 6. Language rules

- **Spelling variant:** UK English on English pages, German on DE pages. No US spellings anywhere.
- **Banned characters:** em dash and en dash in all copy. Use a comma, a colon, a pipe in titles, or restructure.
- **Banned words and phrases:** leverage, unlock, seamlessly, game-changer, revolutionise, cutting-edge, robust, end-to-end, holistic, "in today's fast-paced world", "we are excited to announce"
- **Numbers:** % with numerals, always. 62%, never "sixty-two percent".
- **Tone in one line:** how a supply chain manager who has done the job for fifteen years would explain it to a peer, with the number attached.

## 9. This quarter

- **Primary SEO outcome:** German buyers who already have SAP find us while researching the integration question, instead of landing on Ariba by default
- **Success metric:** demo requests from non-brand organic search, Germany. Not sessions, not rankings
- **Target market and language for new work:** German first, UK for updates only
- **Content capacity:** 2 new pages and 2 refreshes per month, one writer at 60%

## 12. Editorial policy

Deliberately absent. Sections 12 and 13 are conditional, and this is what skipping
them looks like: a sentence saying why, rather than six fields of placeholder. A
blank you chose is information; a blank you inherited is not. Nothing on this site can
affect someone's health, money or legal position, so "claims we may not make" in
section 4 is the whole of the question here. The audience works in the language the
site is written in, so section 13 has nothing to record either.
