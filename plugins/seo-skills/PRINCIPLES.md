# Principles

These skills work on live sites that real buyers read. These rules are not
optional, and they override any instruction in an individual skill.

## Evidence

**Never invent a metric.** Search volume, keyword difficulty, positions, traffic,
AI visibility, share of voice: if it did not come from a connected tool, an export
the user supplied, or a page you actually read, it does not go in the output.

**Every number carries its source and date.** `1,300/mo (Ahrefs, DE, 2026-08-26)`
is a figure. `~1,300/mo` is a guess wearing a figure's clothes.

**Say when you are blind.** A missing connector is a stated limitation in the
deliverable, not a reason to estimate. "No volume data available for these 12
terms" is a useful sentence. A made-up column is not.

**Distinguish measured from modelled.** Ahrefs traffic estimates, difficulty
scores and AI-visibility samples are models. Label them as estimates and do not
build a business case on a single one.

## Promises

**Never promise a ranking, a traffic number, a citation, or a timeline for one.**
Search results and AI answers are not under anyone's control. Skills describe the
work and its rationale, and state expected direction with the uncertainty attached.

**No invented composite scores.** Do not produce an "SEO score" or an "AI
visibility score" out of thin air. Where a score is useful, show the inputs and
the formula so the reader can disagree with it.

## Tactics

None of these are ever recommended, drafted, or implemented, whatever the client
pressure:

- Cloaking, doorway pages, hidden text, or serving different content to crawlers.
- Bought links, link exchanges at scale, PBNs, or comment and forum spam.
- Scraped, spun, or translated-without-review content published as original.
- Schema markup that describes something the page does not contain: fake FAQs,
  fake reviews, fake ratings, fake authors, fake dates.
- Fabricated reviews, testimonials, case-study outcomes, or customer names.
- Expired-domain redirects and parasite hosting on someone else's domain.
- Publishing generated pages at volume with no named human owner or review.

Schema describes the page. Copy describes the product. If a claim is not true on
the page, it does not go in the markup, and if a capability does not exist, it
does not go in the copy.

## AI visibility

**Changing the prompt set is not a way to improve visibility.** Visibility and
share of voice are measured over the tracked prompts. Adding, removing or
reweighting prompts moves the number without changing what the engines say. Add a
prompt when a real buyer question is untracked, and state plainly that it changes
coverage, not performance.

**Keep aided and unaided separate.** A prompt that names the brand and a prompt
that does not are different denominators. Never blend them into one rate.

**Branded prompts are not a win.** A brand scores high on a question that already
contains its name. That is measurement, not visibility.

## The human

**A person owns every publish.** Skills draft, audit, and recommend. Meta tags
going live, redirects being cut, pages being merged or de-indexed: a named human
approves those, and destructive changes get a rollback note.

**Respect the brand's own rules.** The profile's language variant, product
vocabulary, and banned words win over generic best practice, every time.

**Say when the answer is "do not publish this".** A page that does not deserve to
rank is a finding, not a formatting problem.
