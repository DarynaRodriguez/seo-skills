# Site profile

A worked example. The product is fictional, the shape is real: a small consumer app
for people going through one demanding life stage in a country whose language they
do not speak. Free while in pilot, run by two people, with medical and legal claims
strictly off limits.

This exists because the B2B example was the only one for a long time, and the
template's section 3 quietly assumed a buying committee. A tester ran the setup on a
real consumer product and hit that in the first ten minutes. Copy this, replace
everything, keep the blanks honest.

Generated by `/seo-profile-setup` on 2026-08-28. Confirmed by the founder.

## 1. Site

- **Brand name:** Wegweis (always capitalised, never WEGWEIS or wegweis)
- **Primary domain:** wegweis.app
- **CMS / platform:** Next.js on Vercel, server-rendered. Guides come from the CMS, so an unpublished guide is a 404 rather than a thin page
- **Who can publish:** Ana Roth, founder. Anything touching health or legal wording also goes past a named midwife before publish
- **Analytics and search data available:** Google Search Console (wegweis.app, domain property), no paid keyword tool, no AI visibility tracking

## 2. Markets

| Market | Language variant | Domain or path | Primary search engine |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|----------------------|
| Germany | English, UK spelling | wegweis.app/ | google.de |

- **hreflang pairs in use:** none. One language, one path. If German is ever added it needs en and de pairs and self-referencing canonicals
- **Market priority order:** Germany only. Austria and Switzerland get asked about but are not served

## 3. Who this is for

Consumer branch. The business branch is deleted, because there is no buying
committee and pretending otherwise fills the section with false unknowns.

- **Primary audience:** People who moved to Germany as adults and are dealing with a system nobody explained to them, in a language they are still learning
- **Life stage or situation:** The first two years after arrival, or the first year of a major administrative event. It ends: once someone has done it, they stop searching, so this audience replaces itself rather than growing with us
- **Where they are:** Germany, concentrated in Berlin, Munich and Hamburg. Not Germans abroad, and not people planning a move who have not arrived
- **The need state:** "I have a letter I do not understand and a deadline I might already have missed"
- **What disqualifies a visitor:** Fluent German speakers, who are better served by the official sources directly. People outside Germany. Professionals researching the system rather than living in it
- **How they search:** In English, using the German noun for the thing. "Anmeldung appointment", "Elterngeld English", "how to get a Steuernummer". The English word alone rarely appears, and translating the German term out of the query is the fastest way to target a phrase nobody types

**The language they search in is the strategic fact here.** They live in Germany and
search in English using German procedural nouns. That mix decides the whole keyword
set, and it is a much smaller set than the German equivalent, which is a constraint
to plan around rather than a problem to solve.

## 4. What we offer

- **Category we compete in:** Guides for foreigners living in Germany. Not "expat blog", which nobody types
- **What it actually does:** Explains one German administrative or care process at a time, in English, with the forms named and the order of steps fixed. Keeps a checklist of what the reader has already done
- **How it is paid for, if at all:** Free during the pilot. No ads, no affiliate links on procedural pages, and that stays true whatever the traffic does
- **Proof we can cite:** Ana's own paperwork, redacted and photographed. Two named midwives who reviewed the birth and postpartum guides. Official sources for every deadline and figure, linked
- **Claims we may not make:** Anything diagnostic or prescriptive. Anything legal presented as advice rather than as a pointer to the official source or a professional. Any deadline or figure we cannot link to an official page

## 5. Product vocabulary

| Always write | Never write |
|-------------|-------------|
| Wegweis | wegweis, WEGWEIS, "the app" as a name |
| guide | article, post, content piece |
| checklist | tracker, dashboard |
| Anmeldung, Elterngeld, Mutterpass | the English translation on its own, because the German word is what the reader will meet on the form |

Keep the German procedural noun and gloss it in English on first use. Both the
reader and the search query need it.

## 6. Language rules

- **Spelling variant:** UK English
- **Banned characters:** em dash, en dash in body copy
- **Banned words and phrases:** navigate (as a metaphor), journey, seamless, effortless, simply, just, empower, unlock, "in today's world"
- **Numbers:** numerals with % and for money. Euro amounts as EUR 300 in body text
- **Tone in one line:** Like a friend who did it last year and wrote down what went wrong, not a brochure and not a government leaflet

"Simply" and "just" are banned because the process is not simple and the reader
already feels behind.

### Comparison-content policy

- **Comparison pages we will write:** none. There is no product to compare against and it would be tasteless here
- **Decision-support comparisons:** yes, and they are the strongest content on the site. Hospital birth against home birth, statutory against private insurance. Comparisons between options in the world, never between brands
- **Competitors we will not name in copy:** none for legal reasons. Official sources are cited, not framed as competition

## 8. Site structure

- **Pillar pages:** /guides/arriving, /guides/having-a-baby, /guides/money-and-tax
- **Solution / product pages:** /checklist, /about
- **Blog or resources path:** /guides/
- **Pages that must never be indexed:** /checklist/shared/*, which contains personal progress. Also /account/*
- **Known redirect chains or legacy paths:** /blog/* was renamed to /guides/* in 2026. The 301s exist. Two old URLs are still linked from an external forum post and still resolve

## 9. This quarter

- **Primary SEO outcome:** Someone with a letter they do not understand finds the right guide and gets the thing done, rather than missing a deadline
- **Success metric:** Completed checklists, not sessions. Someone who reads and does nothing has not been helped
- **Target market and language for new work:** Germany, English
- **Content capacity:** Two guides a month, both reviewed. That is the real ceiling and briefs should respect it rather than proposing twelve
- **No-go areas:** Anything diagnostic or prescriptive. Any deadline or figure without an official source. Anything that reads as a substitute for a professional

## 10. AI visibility

- **Engines that matter to this buyer:** ChatGPT first, by a distance. This audience asks an assistant before it asks a search engine, because the question is embarrassing to ask a person
- **Tracked prompt set lives in:** nowhere yet. To be created before any AI visibility claim is made
- **Aided prompts and unaided prompts are counted separately.**
- **Sources this buyer trusts:** Official government pages, their own Krankenkasse, and other foreigners who have done it. Not brands

## 12. Editorial policy

- **Evidence standard:** An official source for every deadline, figure and entitlement, linked. A named professional for anything clinical. Nothing rests on a forum post
- **Opinion policy:** Compare, never rank. The site may lay out criteria and say which matters to whom. It may not name a best hospital, insurer or clinic, and "most people choose X" is a ranking wearing a hat
- **Commercial neutrality:** No endorsements, no affiliate links on procedural pages, and that holds whatever the traffic does. If that ever changes, disclosure comes first and this line gets rewritten
- **Professional review policy:** Clinical guides are reviewed by a named midwife. Administrative guides are not systematically reviewed by anyone. **No page may say "reviewed" unless that specific page was**, and the reviewer is named on it
- **User experience policy:** Lived experience is welcome and always marked as one person's experience. "Some people find" is a signpost, never evidence, and never appears in the same sentence as a figure
- **Safety boundaries:** No diagnosis, no dosage, no legal advice. Anything urgent points to the emergency number and the nearest clinic before it explains anything

**Founder authority:** first-hand experience of arriving, registering and going
through the process this site documents. One line, and the About page carries the
rest. It is not material for an article about what to pack.

## 13. Local terminology

| Term | English explanation | Preserve in copy | Search relevance |
|------|--------------------|------------------|------------------|
| Anmeldung | Registering your address | Yes, gloss on first use | Yes, high. "Anmeldung appointment" is a real query |
| Elterngeld | Parental allowance | Yes | Yes, and far larger in German than in English |
| Mutterpass | The maternity record book you are given and must carry | Yes, always | Yes, and there is no English equivalent to translate to |
| Hebamme | Midwife | Yes | Yes, and it is a different query from "midwife" with a different SERP |
| Krankenkasse | Statutory health insurer | Yes | Yes |

- **Preserve or translate:** preserve, and gloss on first use. The reader will meet the German word on a form, a website and a receptionist's desk, and recognising it is part of what this site is for
- **Terms the audience searches in their own language instead:** "health insurance" outranks Krankenkasse in English-language search. Target both, on the same page

## Gaps

- No keyword tool, so every volume is unknown and every ranking is unweighted. The
  briefs say so rather than estimating.
- No AI visibility tracking, while ChatGPT is believed to be the main channel. That
  is the largest gap here and the one worth closing first.
- Whether the two legacy /blog/ URLs still earn anything is unknown until a Search
  Console export is read.
